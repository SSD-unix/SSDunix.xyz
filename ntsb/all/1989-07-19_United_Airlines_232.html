112 FATALITIES 184 SURVIVORS NTSB/AAR-90/06 JULY 19, 1989 MCDONNELL DOUGLAS DC-10-10

United Airlines Flight 232 — The Impossible Landing at Sioux City

Uncontained Stage-1 Fan Disk Burst Leading to Complete Hydraulic System Loss and Pioneer Differential Thrust Control.

Executive Overview: On July 19, 1989, a McDonnell Douglas DC-10-10 (N1819U) operating United Airlines Flight 232 from Denver to Chicago suffered a uncontained catastrophic failure of its tail-mounted #2 engine at FL370. High-velocity titanium fragments severed lines for all three independent hydraulic systems, leaving the primary flight controls completely inoperative. Through unprecedented ingenuity, Crew Resource Management (CRM), and differential engine thrust manipulation assisted by an off-duty check pilot, the crew steered the heavy widebody to Sioux Gateway Airport. Though the aircraft broke apart on impact due to high sink rates, 184 of 296 occupants survived.

Failure Physics & The Metallurgical Root Cause

1. The Metallurgical Origin: Hard-Alpha Inclusion

The NTSB investigation revealed that the Stage-1 titanium fan disk of the GE CF6-6D engine failed due to a fatigue crack that began 18 years earlier during manufacturing in 1971:

  • Double Vacuum Arc Remelting (VAR) Defect: During ingot formation, nitrogen reacted with molten titanium, creating a brittle, nitrogen-rich impurity called a hard-alpha inclusion.
  • Fatigue Growth: Over 15,503 operational cycles, micro-cracks around the inclusion grew slowly. Eventually, a 0.5-inch crack broke open, causing the 8-foot fan assembly to burst uncontainably at 37,000 feet.
  • Inspection Failure: Post-crash dye analysis showed fluorescent dye inside the crack, proving it was present during 6 prior overhauls but went undetected by United Airlines inspectors due to human factors and inadequate non-destructive testing (NDT) procedures.

2. Triple Hydraulic System Destruction

The DC-10 design depended entirely on powered hydraulic actuation with no manual mechanical linkages (manual reversion):

  • System 1, 2, and 3 hydraulic lines converged in close physical proximity near the horizontal stabilizer and tail section.
  • High-energy fan disk shrapnel burst outwards, instantly slicing open lines for all three systems and dumping all hydraulic fluid into the atmosphere within seconds.
  • Without fluid pressure, elevators, rudders, ailerons, and spoilers were left completely limp and floating in the slipstream.

3. Steering via Differential Engine Thrust

Left with zero control surfaces, Check Pilot Denny Fitch kneeled on the cockpit floor, holding one throttle lever in each hand to control the pitch and roll using engine thrust:

  • Roll/Heading Control: Increasing thrust on Engine #1 (left) banked the plane right; increasing thrust on Engine #3 (right) banked the plane left.
  • Pitch Control & Phugoid Cycle: The plane established a 1,500 ft/min phugoid wave. Fitch adjusted both throttles together—throttling up during descents to pitch the nose up, and reducing power near wave peaks to drop the nose.
  • Final Approach Challenge: Without flaps or slats, flight speed was 220 knots (vs 140 normal) with a sink rate of 1,850 ft/min (vs 300 normal). Milliseconds before touchdown, a phugoid wave dropped the right wing, causing initial ground impact.

Industry Impact & Regulatory Legacy

  • Triple Melt VAR Standard: The aviation industry permanently transitioned from Double VAR to Triple Melt Vacuum Arc Remelting for all critical titanium engine rotors to eliminate hard-alpha gas inclusions.
  • Hydraulic Fuses Introduced: Hydraulic fuses were retrofitted into the #3 system in all DC-10 aircraft below the #2 tail engine to automatically isolate severed lines and retain control fluid.
  • Crew Resource Management (CRM): Flight 232 became the absolute gold-standard textbook example of CRM, shifting airline culture away from autocratic captains toward open teamwork, delegation, and clear communication.
  • Child Restraints (Lap Children): The tragic death of a lap infant in the crash sparked decades-long campaigns led by senior attendant Jan Brown Lohr for mandatory child safety seats on commercial aircraft.
