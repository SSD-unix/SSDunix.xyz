7 FATALITIES CATASTROPHIC LOAD SHIFT NTSB/AAR-15/01 APRIL 29, 2013 BOEING 747-428BCF

National Airlines Flight 102 — Bagram Takeoff Crash

Cargo flight from Bagram Airfield (BGR) to Al Maktoum International Airport (DWC) operating under US Air Mobility Command contract.

Accident Summary: On April 29, 2013, at 15:30 local time, a Boeing 747-400 Converted Freighter pitched up uncontrollably seconds after rotation from Runway 03 at Bagram Airfield, entered an aerodynamic stall, and slammed into the ground within the airport perimeter. All 7 crew members perished. Investigation proved an improperly secured 12-ton M-ATV broke free from tie-down straps, rolled aft, smashed through the Aft Pressure Bulkhead, severed hydraulic lines 1 and 2, and destroyed the horizontal stabilizer jackscrew drive mechanism, rendering pitch control impossible.

FDR & Physical Sequence of Failure

T+0s
Rotation initiated at ~4,700 ft down Runway 03. Lift-off occurs at ~6,400 ft.
T+2s
Rear 12-ton M-ATV breaks straps under rotation forces. Vehicle rolls backward off floating G-code pallet.
T+3s
M-ATV smashes into E8 electronics rack (killing FDR/CVR power) and crashes through the Aft Pressure Bulkhead (APB).
T+4s
M-ATV impact severs Hydraulic Systems 1 & 2 lines and strikes the horizontal stabilizer jackscrew drive mechanism, bending it and forcing the stabilizer into a 5° leading-edge-down (extreme pitch-up) mechanical jam.
T+10s
Aircraft pitches up beyond 45°, enters severe aerodynamic stall, banks right, levels out slightly, and crashes 590 ft past runway end.

Critical Technical Takeaways

  • Physics of Strap Failures: National Airlines used an illegal method of dividing cargo weight by a generic reduced strap rating (5,000 lbs reduced to 3,750 lbs), completely ignoring tie-down angle vectors ($30^\circ$ reductions) and floor seat track load limits (max 2,769 lbs per attach point).
  • Structural Destruction vs. CG Shift: NTSB simulations proved that an aft Center of Gravity (CG) shift alone was recoverable by forward control column input. The fatal cause was the physical mechanical destruction of the tail controls and hydraulic lines by the loose 12-ton vehicle.
  • Zero Positive Locks: The heavy military vehicles were loaded on centerline floating pallets without engaging any fixed main deck floor locks, relying 100% on friction and improper polyester web straps.
  • Oversight Deficiencies: FAA inspectors had never conducted direct surveillance on National Airlines' Bagram cargo operations due to U.S. State Department travel restrictions, allowing deficient cargo manuals to persist unchecked.
